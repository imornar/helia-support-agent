# Review of the draft design

Verdict: do not build it as proposed. Each of the six points either moves money without a control, breaks the Finance/Legal audit requirement, or lets the two teams diverge further. Specific problems and what replaces them:

| # | Draft says | What is wrong or risky | What we do instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agent calls Helia APIs directly and issues refunds and plan changes on its own, to be fast | Excessive agency (OWASP LLM06). The model holds credentials for irreversible actions, so one prompt injection in a customer email (LLM01) becomes a real refund. No approver exists, so Finance has no record of who approved what. Refunds are not latency-critical; answers are. | The model has no credentials. It proposes; a deterministic policy engine decides; a human approves writes; a tool gateway is the only component that can call Helia. Speed comes from the read-only path, not from skipping approval. |
| 2 | If the model is unsure, retry the same step up to 10 times | Uncertainty is not a transient error. Re-sampling until something "works" selects for a confident wrong answer (LLM09). Retrying a non-idempotent call can refund twice. Ten retries blow the per-case cost cap (LLM10). | Uncertain means escalate. Only transient tool errors are retried (max 2, backoff, same idempotency key). Invalid model output gets one repair attempt, then escalation. Hard per-case budgets end the loop. |
| 3 | Refund amount and account fields come straight from model output | Improper output handling (LLM05). "Please refund $4,000" in an email, or a poisoned account field, flows straight into a money API. Nothing bounds or checks the value. | Model output is untrusted input. Amounts are recomputed server-side from the charge record (refund cannot exceed the refundable balance of a specific charge). Fields are schema-validated with enums and ranges. The model picks *which* charge, never *how much*. |
| 4 | Each team wires tools as needed; standardise later | "Later" never comes. Two auth models, two logging formats, and two approval behaviours ship, and every new tool widens the gap. Leadership asked for one approach now. | One gateway, one tool manifest format, one versioned policy bundle from day one. Teams own their tools and rules; the platform owns the rails. Unregistered tools are rejected by the gateway. |
| 5 | Log the final reply so support can see it | Fails the compliance requirement: no record of inputs, proposed action, policy decision, approver, model/prompt version, or execution result. Cannot debug, reconcile, or roll back. Raw logs may also leak PII (LLM02). | Append-only audit event for every step, with approver identity, policy rule ID, and versions. PII is redacted in logs. Finance reconciles every agent refund against an approval record nightly. |
| 6 | Launch to all customers on day one | Maximum blast radius, no baseline to compare against, no kill switch, and the human console is not proven to work alongside the agent. | Shadow, then assist (humans approve everything), then earned autonomy per action type behind metric gates. Kill switches per tool, team, and phase, with the existing console as the fallback. |
